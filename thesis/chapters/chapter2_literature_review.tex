\section{Review Scope}
\label{sec:lit-scope}

This work draws on four lines of research that are usually studied separately: causal rationale discovery in graph learning, Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models on graphs, learning under distribution shift, and data-driven weather forecasting. It also builds on causal discovery for climate time series and on tropical intraseasonal dynamics. This chapter reviews each line, compares the closest works in Table~\ref{tab:lit}, and states the research gap.

\section{Tropical Intraseasonal Variability and OLR}
\label{sec:lit-bsiso}

Organised tropical convection varies on intraseasonal time scales of 30--60 days. Madden and Julian~\cite{madden1971detection} discovered this variability as an eastward-propagating oscillation in the tropical winds, now known as the MJO. In boreal summer the dominant mode is the BSISO, whose convective envelope forms over the equatorial Indian Ocean and propagates northward over India and the Bay of Bengal and eastward toward the western Pacific, producing a characteristic tilted rain band. Wang and Xie~\cite{wang1997model} explained this structure as an equatorial Kelvin--Rossby wave packet modified by the monsoon mean flow. Goswami and Ajaya Mohan~\cite{goswami2001intraseasonal} linked these oscillations to the active and break spells of the Indian monsoon, which makes them central to subseasonal prediction over India~\cite{vitart2017s2s}. Lee \emph{et al.}~\cite{lee2013realtime} defined real-time BSISO indices from a combined empirical orthogonal function analysis of OLR and 850\,hPa zonal wind over the Asian monsoon region; this thesis reproduces their recipe to define the active season.

Embedded in the envelope are convectively coupled equatorial waves. Kelvin waves coupled to convection travel eastward at roughly 15--20\ms\ and faster when dry~\cite{kiladis2009convectively}. Signals can therefore cross thousands of kilometres within one or two days, so a purely geographic cutoff on candidate drivers would remove genuine dependencies. These speed scales underlie the physical lag mask of Chapter~\ref{chap:validation}.

OLR has been observed continuously by satellites since the 1970s~\cite{liebmann1996description}. Its relation to cold-cloud fraction and large-scale convective rainfall~\cite{arkin1987relationship} makes it the standard observable of tropical convection and one of the two fields of the BSISO index.

\section{Causal Rationales in Graph Learning}
\label{sec:lit-dir}

Wu \emph{et al.}~\cite{wu2022discovering} proposed Discovering Invariant Rationales (DIR) for graph neural networks. A rationale generator splits each input graph into a causal part and a non-causal part. An intervener then swaps the non-causal part between training instances, creating several interventional "environments" for the same causal part. The model is trained to minimise both the mean and the variance of its risk across these environments: if a prediction stays stable when the non-causal part is replaced, the retained part carries invariant, causal information. DIR was designed for graph classification, for example predicting a property of a molecule. It does not address spatiotemporal forecasting, and it provides no mechanism for adapting the model when the data distribution changes.

The splitter used in this thesis adapts the DIR mechanism to regression on a fixed spatial graph. For a target region on a given day, the candidate source regions form the graph, and the non-causal sources are swapped with the same sources on other days (Section~\ref{sec:moe-splitter}).

\section{Mixture of Experts on Graphs}
\label{sec:lit-moe}

The Mixture of Experts combines several specialised networks through a gating function that assigns input-dependent weights~\cite{jacobs1991adaptive}. Sparse gating, which activates only a few experts per input, made the approach scale to very large models~\cite{shazeer2017outrageously}. In its general form, a mixture of $K$ experts $E_k$ with a gate $g$ predicts
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{k=1}^{K} g_k(\mathbf{x})\,E_k(\mathbf{x}),\qquad g(\mathbf{x})=\mathrm{softmax}\big(\mathrm{TopK}(\mathbf{W}_g\mathbf{x})\big),
  \label{eq:moe}
\end{equation}
where TopK keeps the $k$ largest gate logits and sets the rest to $-\infty$, so that only $k$ experts are evaluated. The gate decides \emph{which} expert answers, and the experts decide \emph{what} the answer is. Graph MoE methods differ mainly in what the gate sees and in how the expert pool changes over time. Three recent graph MoE methods are closest to this work.

\paragraph{Topology-conditioned experts.} Ghaffari \emph{et al.}~\cite{ghaffari2026graph} forecast traffic with a small router that blends several frozen, pre-trained graph neural network experts at each node, using the node's topology and recent input. The router is cheap and effective, but the experts are frozen: they are never retrained or spawned, so the method has no response to drift.

\paragraph{Curvature-guided routing.} Cao \emph{et al.}~\cite{cao2026geometric} route each node to expert sub-networks according to its Ollivier--Ricci curvature, operating across Riemannian spaces. Curvature captures local geometry well, but it is a static property of the graph and carries no notion of drift or causality.

\paragraph{Experts that grow over time.} Kong \emph{et al.}~\cite{kong2025dynamic} proposed a dynamic MoE for incremental graph learning that adds a new expert whenever a new batch of data arrives, with a regularisation term to limit forgetting. Experts are added on a schedule, whether or not the data have actually changed.

\section{Distribution Shift on Graphs}
\label{sec:lit-ood}

Out-of-distribution generalisation on graphs studies models whose test distribution differs from the training distribution in features, structure or labels~\cite{li2022ood}. GraphMETRO~\cite{wu2024graphmetro} addresses complex shifts with a mixture of aligned experts: each expert is trained to be invariant to one hand-constructed type of shift, and a learned gate blends them. The approach is effective when the shift types are known, but the shift components are hand-constructed and static, and the method does not discover shift-relevant structure from the data. In the streaming setting, concept-drift methods detect changes in an error or statistic stream~\cite{gama2014survey}, for example with ADWIN~\cite{bifet2007learning}, and climate science uses sequential tests such as STARS~\cite{rodionov2004sequential} to detect regime shifts. Adapting a model after a shift risks catastrophic forgetting of earlier regimes~\cite{kirkpatrick2017overcoming}.

\section{Causal Discovery for Climate Time Series}
\label{sec:lit-pcmci}

Granger causality~\cite{granger1969investigating} asks whether the past of one series improves the prediction of another. In climate data, lagged correlation and naive Granger tests are distorted by autocorrelation and common drivers~\cite{runge2014quantifying}. PCMCI~\cite{runge2019detecting} addresses this in two stages: a fast condition-selection step finds a small superset of each target's parents, and a momentary conditional-independence (MCI) test then checks each candidate while conditioning on the parents of both the target and the source. PCMCI is the reference method for causal discovery in Earth-system time series~\cite{runge2019inferring,runge2018causal}; extensions such as LPCMCI handle latent confounders~\cite{gerhardus2020high}. It has been used to study monsoon teleconnections~\cite{dicapua2020tropical}, typically over tens of pre-selected indices rather than hundreds of regional nodes, and the discovered graph is usually an end in itself rather than an input to a forecasting model.

Because thousands of candidate links are tested, multiple-testing control is essential; the Benjamini--Hochberg procedure controls the false discovery rate~\cite{benjamini1995controlling}. Surrogate data, series that keep chosen statistical properties of the real data but destroy their coupling~\cite{theiler1992testing,schreiber2000surrogate}, provide a direct test of whether a discovery procedure selects spurious sources.

\section{Data-Driven Weather Forecasting}
\label{sec:lit-weather}

GraphCast~\cite{lam2023learning} showed that a graph neural network trained on reanalysis can outperform operational medium-range forecasting on most variables and leads, and limited-area versions extend the idea to regional domains~\cite{oskarsson2023graph}. These models learn dependencies between regions implicitly. They do not expose a tested statement of which regions drive a target, and they do not adapt when those dependencies change.

\section{Research Gap}
\label{sec:lit-gap}

Existing studies address causal rationale discovery, graph distribution shifts, dynamic expert learning and graph-based weather forecasting separately. They do not combine causal structure discovery with drift-triggered expert adaptation for spatiotemporal weather forecasting. Specifically:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{G\arabic*.}]
  \item \textbf{Causal source selection is not validated.} Causal rationales in DIR are learned end-to-end and never checked independently; causal graphs from PCMCI are checked but not used to guide a forecaster.
  \item \textbf{Experts do not respond to measured drift.} Graph MoE methods route by topology or curvature (GC-MoE, GeoMoE), keep experts frozen (GC-MoE), use hand-constructed shift types (GraphMETRO), or add experts on a fixed schedule (DyMoE).
  \item \textbf{Drift is not detected through causal structure.} Concept-drift detectors watch error streams, which react only after forecasts have already degraded.
\end{enumerate}
This thesis addresses the gap by validating causal drivers first, using them to guide regional experts, and adapting only the regions whose behaviour has measurably changed.
