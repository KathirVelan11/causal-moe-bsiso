\section{Forecasting Task}
\label{sec:data-task}

Let $Y^r_t$ be the standardised OLR anomaly of region $r\in\{0,\dots,49\}$ on day $t$. For lead $L$ days, a model for region $r$ predicts
\begin{equation}
  \hat{Y}^{r}_{t+L} = f_{r}\big(\{X^{i}_{t-\ell}: i\in\mathcal{N}_r,\ \ell\in\Lambda\}\big),
  \label{eq:task}
\end{equation}
where $X^i_t$ is the standardised anomaly of node $i$, $\mathcal{N}_r$ the nodes of the source regions selected for region $r$, and $\Lambda$ the input lags. Forecasts are \emph{direct} (one model per lead, no roll-out); the primary lead is $L=7$ days and the secondary lead $L=1$ day. The central question is how $\mathcal{N}_r$ should be chosen. A forecast is scored only if its target day lies in the BSISO season $\mathcal{S}$ and in the test period 2013--2022; inputs may come from any earlier day.

\section{Dataset}
\label{sec:data-provenance}

The data are BSISO monitoring fields on a 2.5\dg\ grid (25 latitudes from 30\dg N to 30\dg S $\times$ 144 longitudes, i.e. 3{,}600 cells of about 275\,km), daily from 1 January 1979 to 31 December 2022: 16{,}071 days with none missing. Each field was anomaly-processed upstream, with the seasonal cycle of each location and calendar day removed (climatology base period 1979--2014) and the anomalies normalised per grid cell. Table~\ref{tab:variables} lists the six variables.

Single grid cells are too noisy and too numerous for a 60-lag causal analysis, so the cells are grouped into 50 regions by $k$-means clustering of their OLR time series over the development period; 46 of the 50 regions are contiguous, with about 72 cells each. A region adjacency graph, built from which regions touch on the map with longitude wrap-around, defines the local candidate pools of the forecaster. Each node $i=(r,v)$ is the mean of variable $v$ over region $r$ (SST over ocean cells only), re-standardised with development-period statistics:
\begin{equation}
  X^{i}_t=\frac{Z^{i}_t-\mu_i}{\sigma_i},\qquad \mu_i,\sigma_i\ \text{computed on 1979--2012}.
  \label{eq:standardise}
\end{equation}
With 50 regions and six variables there are $N=300$ nodes (Figure~\ref{fig:nodes}). For each OLR target, every node at every lag from 1 to 60 days is a candidate source: $300\times60=18{,}000$ candidates per target and 900{,}000 for all 50 targets. For the resolution check, each region is split into two by $k$-means, giving 100 regions and 596 nodes (four sub-regions have no ocean cells and therefore no SST node).

The forecaster describes each region on each day by a 49-value feature vector: the six variables at lags $\Lambda=\{0,1,2,3,5,7,10\}$ days, plus the region's ocean fraction.

\section{BSISO Season}
\label{sec:data-season}

The BSISO is a summer phenomenon, so target days are restricted to its active season, defined from the oscillation's own amplitude rather than a fixed calendar window. The index follows Lee \emph{et al.}~\cite{lee2013realtime}: OLR and u850 anomalies over 10\dg S--30\dg N, 40\dg--160\dg E (the grid ends at 30\dg N), with the preceding 120-day mean removed, $\sqrt{\cos\varphi}$ area weighting, and a combined EOF fitted on May--October days of 1979--2012 only. The amplitude is $A_t=\sqrt{\mathrm{PC1}_t^2+\mathrm{PC2}_t^2}$. In each year, the season runs from the first to the last stretch of at least 20 days in which the 31-day mean amplitude exceeds 1, padded by 21 days at each end. The season averages 208 days per year (range 82--286), giving 7{,}153 discovery days (1979--2012) and 1{,}989 scored test days (2013--2022).

The season restricts \emph{target} days only. A source at lag 45 for a target on 1 June is read from mid-April; deleting non-season days before building lags would remove precisely the precursor information under study.

\section{Development and Test Split and Leakage Control}
\label{sec:data-split}

Everything that shapes the graph or the models uses 1979--2012 only (Figure~\ref{fig:timesplit}, Table~\ref{tab:leakage}): node standardisation, the regional partition, the BSISO EOF, PCMCI and its validation, and the choice of $k$. The forecaster trains on 1979--2004 and validates on 2005--2012. The graph is frozen before 2013--2022 is used, and the test years are used only for scoring. A chronological split is used because random splits of autocorrelated series place near-duplicate days in both training and test sets. The upstream anomaly climatology (1979--2014) overlaps the test period by two years; all compared models share it, and refitting it on 1979--2012 is listed as future work.

\section{Chapter Summary}

The data are six daily variables on a tropical grid, grouped into 50 OLR-coherent regions and 300 nodes. Targets are BSISO-season OLR anomalies at 7-day and 1-day leads, scored on held-out 2013--2022 days, with all modelling choices made on 1979--2012.
