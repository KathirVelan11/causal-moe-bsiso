\label{chap:phase2}

Phase~1 tested the causal graph and the adaptive experts on a deliberately controlled problem: one convective proxy, daily data and 50 large regions. Phase~2 carries the components that passed those tests into an operationally relevant setting: hourly, kilometre-scale forecasting of near-surface weather over India. This chapter specifies the design; its experiments are future work.

\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:p2-motivation}

The BSISO studied in Phase~1 drives the active and break spells of the Indian summer monsoon~\cite{goswami2001intraseasonal}, so the large-scale drivers identified there are physically relevant to India. High-resolution regional reanalysis~\cite{rani2021imdaa} and dense station records~\cite{smith2011integrated} make hourly verification possible, and near-surface heat, humidity and wind over India have strong societal impact. Their predictability changes with monsoon regime and ENSO state, which makes adaptation to shift a requirement. The Phase~2 horizon of up to 72 hours also matches the one- to two-day time scale of the dependencies found in Phase~1.

\paragraph{1--2. Data and preprocessing.} IMDAA provides hourly regional reanalysis at about 12\,km as the primary input and target; ERA5~\cite{hersbach2020era5} provides the boundary context and candidate large-scale drivers (BSISO and ENSO indices, remote circulation); and the NOAA Integrated Surface Database provides station observations. Fields are regridded, hourly time stamps aligned, and normalisation statistics estimated on training years only. Targets are 2\,m temperature and relative humidity, 10\,m wind components and mean sea-level pressure on the grid, plus 2\,m temperature and dewpoint at stations.

\paragraph{3. Causal detector.} The Phase~1 pipeline (PCMCI, decoys, speed mask, persistence) is applied with one change suggested by the Phase~1 results: for a forecast at lead $L$, drivers are tested only at lags $\tau\ge L$, so that every retained link is usable at the issue time.

\paragraph{4--5. Shared backbone, causal gate and regional experts.} A shared graph neural network backbone encodes the regional state by message passing on the regional mesh~\cite{lam2023learning,oskarsson2023graph}. Region-specific experts refine its output, and a sparse gate~\cite{shazeer2017outrageously} weights the experts using the backbone state together with the validated drivers $\mathbf{c}$:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\mathbf{y}}_{n,t+L}=D(\mathbf{z}_{n,t})+\sum_{k} g_k(\mathbf{z}_{n,t},\mathbf{c}_{n,t})\,E_k(\mathbf{z}_{n,t}),\qquad \textstyle\sum_k g_k=1 .
\end{equation}
In Phase~1 the graph selected \emph{predictors}; in Phase~2 it also conditions the \emph{gate}. A driver too slow to add direct predictive information can still identify the regime, for example an active BSISO phase over India, in which a particular expert is reliable.

\paragraph{6--8. Forecasting, validation and benchmarking.} Forecasts are rolled out autoregressively to 72 hours, verified against station observations by RMSE, MAE and bias per variable, lead and region, and benchmarked against GraphCast~\cite{lam2023learning} and WeatherNext using the same data, horizons and protocol. Scores are also stratified by monsoon phase and ENSO state.

\paragraph{9--10. Drift monitoring and selective adaptation.} Each region runs the dual-channel detector of Phase~1. Because the PCMCI parent-set distance proved noisy over short windows, the structure channel uses continuous signals: the splitter-score pattern, which detected shifts early in Phase~1, or strength-based distances over longer windows. Only the affected region's expert is retrained, spawned or reactivated, with pooled reactivation as the default.

\section{Evaluation under Distribution Shift}
\label{sec:p2-eval}

The evaluation keeps the Phase~1 discipline of a chronological holdout, frozen graphs and matched controls (a random or correlation context in place of the causal one). It adds regime-stratified scores (El Ni\~{n}o, La Ni\~{n}a and neutral years; monsoon onset, active and break phases), leave-one-regime-out tests, and adaptation metrics: detection latency, false-alarm rate, error after adaptation, forgetting, and compute cost compared with full retraining.

\section{Status and Path to Global Scale}
\label{sec:p2-status}

The causal-discovery and validation pipeline, the drift channels and the expert archive with pooled reactivation are implemented and tested on Phase~1 data. The India datasets, the shared backbone and the causal gate are designed, and their open choices (backbone architecture, and regional, per-node or regime experts) will be settled on validation data. Once the regional system is competitive, the same architecture extends to global scale: larger meshes, more diverse regimes, long-range teleconnections and a larger expert pool. At that scale causal discovery will need hierarchical candidate sets and the expert pool active management.
