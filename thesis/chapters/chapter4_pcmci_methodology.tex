\section{Phase 1 Workflow}
\label{sec:pcmci-overview}

Figure~\ref{fig:phase1-workflow} shows the complete Phase~1 workflow. The data (step~1) are reduced to 50 regions and 300 nodes (step~2), and the BSISO season and time split are fixed (step~3). Stage~A discovers a lagged causal graph with PCMCI (step~4); Stage~B validates it with four independent checks (step~5); and the graph is merged into a region-level prior and frozen (step~6). Stage~C uses the prior to guide a causal splitter and per-region expert (step~7), which produce the OLR forecast (step~8). Forecast errors and causal-structure changes feed a dual-channel drift detector (step~9) that decides when a region's expert is retrained, archived, reactivated or replaced (step~10). Stage~D tracks whether the causal structure itself changes over time (step~11). This chapter describes Stage~A; Chapter~\ref{chap:validation} describes Stages~B--D and the expert lifecycle.

\section{Why Correlation Is Not Enough}
\label{sec:pcmci-why}

Three mechanisms make a source correlated with a target without influencing it directly. \emph{Autocorrelation}: a source that truly acts at lag 2 also appears correlated at lags 1, 3 and 4 because it is persistent. \emph{Common drivers}: an active BSISO phase changes winds, moisture and OLR over large areas at once, so two regions appear linked through their shared driver. \emph{Mediation}: a source acting through an intermediate region appears linked to the target directly. In each case the spurious dependence disappears once the right variables are conditioned on. Conditional independence, written $X\indep Y\mid Z$, formalises this idea, and causal discovery tests it systematically~\cite{runge2018causal}.

\section{Partial-Correlation Test}
\label{sec:pcmci-parcorr}

The conditional-independence test is partial correlation (ParCorr). Given samples of $x$, $y$ and a conditioning matrix $\mathbf{Z}$ with orthonormal basis $\mathbf{Q}$, the residuals $\mathbf{r}_x=(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}^\top)\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{r}_y=(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}^\top)\mathbf{y}$ give
\begin{equation}
  \hat\rho(x,y\mid\mathbf{Z})=\frac{\mathbf{r}_x^{\top}\mathbf{r}_y}{\lVert\mathbf{r}_x\rVert\,\lVert\mathbf{r}_y\rVert},\qquad
  t=\hat\rho\sqrt{\frac{n-2-|\mathbf{Z}|}{1-\hat\rho^2}},
  \label{eq:parcorr}
\end{equation}
where $t$ follows a Student-$t$ distribution with $n-2-|\mathbf{Z}|$ degrees of freedom under the null hypothesis $\rho=0$. ParCorr detects linear dependence.

\section{Stage 1: PC1 Condition Selection}
\label{sec:pcmci-pc1}

For each OLR target $X^j_t$, all 18{,}000 candidates $X^i_{t-\tau}$ ($\tau=1,\dots,60$) start in the parent set. At iteration $\ell$, each candidate is tested for $X^i_{t-\tau}\indep X^j_t\mid\mathbf{S}_\ell$, where $\mathbf{S}_\ell$ holds the $\ell$ strongest other candidates found so far, and it is dropped if the $p$-value exceeds $\alpha_{\mathrm{PC}}=0.05$. Iterations continue while enough candidates remain. Because the strongest drivers, typically the target's own recent past, are conditioned on first, most autocorrelation-induced and indirect candidates are removed in the first few rounds. The result is a small superset $\hat{\mathcal{P}}(X^j_t)$ of the true parents. Non-target nodes are screened only against their own past, which gives the autocorrelation structure needed in the next stage.

\section{Stage 2: Momentary Conditional Independence}
\label{sec:pcmci-mci}

Each survivor is retested while conditioning on the target's other parents \emph{and} on the source's own parents, shifted by the lag:
\begin{equation}
  \hat\rho^{\,\mathrm{MCI}}_{i\to j}(\tau)=\hat\rho\Big(X^i_{t-\tau},\,X^j_t\;\Big|\;\hat{\mathcal{P}}(X^j_t)\setminus\{X^i_{t-\tau}\},\ \hat{\mathcal{P}}(X^i_{t-\tau})\Big).
  \label{eq:mci}
\end{equation}
Conditioning on the target's parents removes indirect paths and common drivers among them. Conditioning on the source's parents removes the source's own memory, so the test measures the information the source adds at lag $\tau$ beyond its history. This calibrates the test for strongly autocorrelated climate data and sharpens the lag estimate~\cite{runge2019detecting}. The value $\hat\rho^{\,\mathrm{MCI}}$ is used as the link strength.

\paragraph{A worked example.} Suppose region A's OLR drives region B's OLR at lag 2, A is strongly persistent, and an active BSISO phase raises the moisture of both regions simultaneously. The lagged correlation between A and B is then significant at lags 1--5, and region A's moisture also appears correlated with B. PC1 conditions on B's own recent past and on A at lag 2, the strongest candidate, which removes most of the neighbouring lags. MCI then conditions A at lag 3 on A's own past at lags 4 and beyond: the remaining dependence at lag 3 is merely A's persistence, so the test is not significant. Conditioning on B's moisture parent removes the shared moisture signal. Only the direct link at lag 2 survives, with a strength that measures A's unique contribution.

\section{False-Discovery-Rate Control}
\label{sec:pcmci-fdr}

Testing 18{,}000 candidates per target at $\alpha=0.05$ would admit about 900 false links per target even if none existed. The Benjamini--Hochberg procedure~\cite{benjamini1995controlling} controls the expected fraction of false links among those selected. With $m=18{,}000$ sorted $p$-values $p_{(1)}\le\dots\le p_{(m)}$,
\begin{equation}
  q_{(h)}=\min_{h'\ge h}\ \min\!\Big(1,\ \frac{m}{h'}\,p_{(h')}\Big),
  \label{eq:bh}
\end{equation}
and a link is retained if $q\le0.05$. Candidates dropped by PC1 count with $p=1$, so the correction covers all 18{,}000 hypotheses. Lag 0 is excluded, so every link points forward in time.

\section{Vectorised Implementation}
\label{sec:pcmci-compute}

The reference Tigramite implementation builds a new data array for every test, which is too slow at 18{,}000 candidates per target. Our implementation (Algorithm~\ref{alg:pcmci}) builds the lagged design matrix of all candidates once, shares it across the 50 targets, and tests all candidates that share a conditioning set in one batched projection, a single QR decomposition followed by one matrix product. It was verified against Tigramite on synthetic data with known parents, including a common driver and a noise node: the PC1 parent sets are identical, and the MCI values agree to within $2\times10^{-3}$.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Vectorised PCMCI for one OLR target}
\label{alg:pcmci}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require node series $\mathbf{X}$; season days $t\in\mathcal{S}$, 1979--2012; target $j$; $\tau\in[1,60]$; $\alpha_{\mathrm{PC}}=\alpha_{\mathrm{FDR}}=0.05$
\State Build design $\mathbf{D}$ with one column per candidate $(i,\tau)$ \Comment{shared by all targets}
\State $C\gets$ all 18{,}000 candidates; $\ell\gets0$
\While{$|C|-1\ge \ell$} \Comment{PC1}
  \State $\mathbf{S}\gets$ the $\ell$ strongest members of $C$; orthonormalise $\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{S}}$ by QR
  \State residualise the target and all of $\mathbf{D}_C$ in one product; compute $\hat\rho$ and $p$-values (Eq.~\ref{eq:parcorr})
  \State drop candidates with $p>\alpha_{\mathrm{PC}}$; $\ell\gets \ell+1$
\EndWhile
\ForAll{$(i,\tau)\in C$} \Comment{MCI}
  \State test $X^i_{t-\tau}\indep X^j_t$ given the target's other parents and the source's lagged parents (Eq.~\ref{eq:mci})
\EndFor
\State compute $q$ over all 18{,}000 candidates (Eq.~\ref{eq:bh}) and keep links with $q\le\alpha_{\mathrm{FDR}}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Assumptions}
\label{sec:pcmci-assumptions}

PCMCI identifies causal parents under causal sufficiency (all common drivers are observed), stationarity, and faithfulness. In regional climate data these hold only approximately: the BSISO phase, ENSO and sub-regional moisture processes are only partly represented by 300 region means; dependence varies between years; and convection responds nonlinearly to moisture. The fields within one region also move together, so which field PCMCI selects can vary even when the source region is stable. For these reasons the graph is treated as a hypothesis to be validated (Chapter~\ref{chap:validation}), and claims are made at the level of source regions. Methods that admit latent confounders, such as LPCMCI~\cite{gerhardus2020high}, are a natural extension.
