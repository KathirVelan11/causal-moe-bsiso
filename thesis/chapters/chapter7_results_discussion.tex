\section{BSISO Index and Season Diagnostics}
\label{sec:res-season}

The BSISO index reproduced from the project's OLR and u850 anomalies (Section~\ref{sec:data-season}) has leading modes that explain 11.2\% and 7.7\% of the combined variance on May--October days of 1979--2012. Lee \emph{et al.}~\cite{lee2013realtime} report approximately 10\% and 7\% for their BSISO1 modes. The small excess is consistent with the smaller domain used here (northern edge at 30\dg N instead of 40\dg N), which concentrates the analysis on the region of strongest intraseasonal convection. Table~\ref{tab:bsiso} gives the comparison. The resulting amplitude has the expected seasonal cycle: low in boreal winter and spring, rising above 1 in May and remaining high until about November (Figure~\ref{fig:season}, top).

\section{Stage A: PCMCI Discovery (RQ1)}
\label{sec:res-stagea}

\subsection{Size and composition of the graph}
PCMCI at an FDR of 0.05 retains \textbf{536 links} into the 50 OLR targets, 10.7 per target on average, out of 900{,}000 candidates. The graph is therefore very sparse: about 0.06\% of candidates survive. Table~\ref{tab:stagea} breaks the links down.

Three features stand out.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Most links are within the target's own region}, and most of these are the target's own OLR at lags of 1--7 days. This is the expected autoregressive structure of convection: the best single predictor of a region's OLR is its own recent past.
  \item \textbf{Outside links are short-lag.} The median lag is one day and 90\% are at two days or less. No outside link has a lag of 25 days or more. Slow precursors, such as SST one BSISO cycle ahead, do not survive. This is consistent with how PCMCI works: once the target's short-lag parents are conditioned on, the information that a slow precursor carries about the target has largely been transmitted through those faster intermediaries, so the precursor has little \emph{direct} link left. This is a property of conditional rather than marginal dependence, not evidence that slow precursors are irrelevant.
  \item \textbf{There are no outside SST links.} The atmospheric fields respond to SST over days to weeks, and these responses are captured by the atmospheric nodes themselves. SST's direct contribution, after conditioning on them, is too small to pass FDR.
\end{enumerate}
Among the outside drivers, OLR is the most frequent field, followed by the low-level wind u850, the upper-level wind u200 and the low-level height h850. This ordering matches the physical picture of propagating convection: convection in a neighbouring region, together with the low-level flow that carries moisture and the upper-level outflow and wave response, precede convection in the target.

\subsection{Spatial structure}
Figure~\ref{fig:graphmap} maps the validated outside drivers. Most links connect neighbouring regions, along the equatorial Indian Ocean and Maritime Continent, across the western Pacific and across the southern subtropical Pacific. A handful of longer links, such as 22$\to$32, 46$\to$28 and 8$\to$38, are carried by wind and height fields and require 1--2 days at equatorial-wave speeds. The map also shows which links reappear at 100 regions (Section~\ref{sec:res-b4}).

\subsection{A worked example: region 22}
Region 22 lies over the equatorial western Pacific near the Maritime Continent, where several circulation regimes meet. Table~\ref{tab:region22} follows its links through the pipeline. PCMCI finds 13 links: ten within the region (its own OLR at lags 1--3 and 5--7, and its u850 and h850 at lags 1--2) and three outside links (h850 from region 27 at lags 1 and 3, and PW from region 6 at lag 2). Both source regions are about 280\,km away at their closest cells, so the implied speeds of 1--3\ms\ are physically easy. The resulting prior is region 27 (strength 0.21) and region 6 (strength 0.05). Both region-27 links persist at 100 regions; the region-6 PW link does not and is flagged, giving a persistence score of 0.67.

\subsection{Answer to RQ1}
PCMCI identifies a sparse set of conditional lagged dependencies whose fields, lags and geography are physically interpretable. The answer to RQ1 is positive, with the qualification that the identified dependencies are dominated by short-lag, largely regional coupling rather than by long-lead teleconnections.

\subsection{B1: decoys}
\label{sec:res-b1}
In the separate pass with 250 phase-randomised decoys in the pool (550 nodes, 33{,}000 candidates per target), \textbf{no decoy is selected as a parent of any target}. The decoy rate is 0\%, far below the 30\% warning line. At the same time, the real part of the graph is almost unchanged: 517 real links (10.3 per target) compared with 536 (10.7 per target) in the clean pass. The small reduction is expected, because the larger pool increases the number of hypotheses in the BH correction and slightly raises the effective threshold. Figure~\ref{fig:b1} shows that the strength distributions of real links in the two passes are nearly identical, including the cluster of strong own-OLR links near $|\MCI|\approx 0.85$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.78\textwidth]{04_b1_decoys.png}
\caption{B1: distribution of $|\MCI|$ for real links in the clean pass (536 links) and in the pass with 250 decoys in the pool (517 links). No decoy was selected, so the decoy rate is 0\%.}
\label{fig:b1}
\end{figure}

Because no decoy survives FDR, the strength bar of Eq.~\eqref{eq:kappa} is zero and removes no real link. The evidence from B1 is therefore carried by the decoy rate and by the stability of the real graph. The result shows that the pipeline does not select series merely because they share the autocorrelation and the 30--60-day spectral peak of real climate nodes. Within the limits of the surrogate null (Section~\ref{sec:val-b1}), this addresses the most common source of spurious links in climate data.

\subsection{B2: physical lag mask}
\label{sec:res-b2}
Only \textbf{3 of 536} links need a speed above the limit of their source field: h850 from region 22 to region 32 (5{,}531\,km at lag 1, 64\ms), OLR from region 28 to region 33 (2{,}889\,km at lag 1, 33\ms) and OLR from region 3 to region 47 (2{,}215\,km at lag 1, 26\ms). All three are at lag 1, which is where the speed requirement is highest. They are removed from the prior and kept, flagged, in the graph file. Of the outside links that pass, 28 span 500--5{,}500\,km, and the fastest of them requires 46\ms; all such fast links come from the wind and height fields, consistent with equatorial-wave propagation. Figure~\ref{fig:b2} shows that the implied speeds of OLR and PW links lie mostly below 5\ms, the speed of the BSISO envelope, while dynamical fields reach faster speeds at larger distances. No link reaches the 25-day oscillation flag.

\subsection{B3: top-\texorpdfstring{$k$}{k} selection}
\label{sec:res-b3}
The validation $R^2$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:r2k}, averaged over targets, increases monotonically with $k$, from 0.9619 with own-region links only to 0.9633 with ten outside links (Figure~\ref{fig:b3}). The gain from each additional outside link decreases from about $3.6\times10^{-4}$ for the first to below $10^{-5}$ for the ninth and tenth. The maximum is at $k^\ast=10$, which equals the largest number of outside links of any target, so B3 retains all 157 outside links that passed B2.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{06_b3_k.png}
\caption{B3: mean validation $R^2$ on 2005--2012 season days (fit 1979--2004) as a function of the number $k$ of outside links per target (left), and the gain from the $k$-th link (right). Own-region links are always included, so $R^2$ starts high.}
\label{fig:b3}
\end{figure}

Two observations explain this outcome. First, PCMCI with FDR is already sparse: with about three outside links per target on average, there is little to prune. Second, the high baseline $R^2$ shows that own-region links, evaluated at their own lags, explain most of the target's variance, and every outside link adds a small amount of independent information on development data. B3 therefore confirms that no target has an excessive number of drivers and fixes the prior at its natural size of \textbf{2.06 source regions per target}, distributed as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:checks} (right): 2 targets have no outside driver (regions 13 and 42), 13 have one, 24 have two, and 11 have three to five.

\subsection{B4: resolution persistence}
\label{sec:res-b4}
At 100 regions, the mean persistence score across the 48 targets that have outside drivers is \textbf{0.62}, and \textbf{33 of 48} targets reach the 0.5 threshold (Figure~\ref{fig:b4}). Of the 157 outside links, 97 reappear at the finer resolution. Persistence differs markedly by source field: OLR links persist best (56 of 70), followed by u200 (14 of 22), u850 (17 of 37), h850 (6 of 17) and PW (4 of 11).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{07_b4_persistence.png}
\caption{B4: persistence score of each target at 100 regions, sorted (left; orange: below 0.5, flagged), and the share of links that persist by source field (right). Targets 13 and 42 have no outside driver and are not scored.}
\label{fig:b4}
\end{figure}

The field dependence is physically sensible. Links between convective regions (OLR to OLR) reflect the large-scale propagation of convective envelopes and survive a change of partition. Upper-level wind anomalies are large in scale and also persist well. Moisture (PW) and low-level height links are more local and noisier at sub-region scale, so they are more sensitive to how regions are drawn. Seven targets have a persistence score of zero; these are mostly targets with one or two weak outside links.

\subsection{Answer to RQ2}
The graph passes the decoy test with no decoy selected, the physical-speed test with 3 failures in 536 links, and the persistence test on average with 62\% of outside links reappearing at doubled resolution. B3 confirms that the graph is already compact. Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:h1} and~\ref{hyp:h2} are supported. The answer to RQ2 is that the discovered relationships are robust under every check that can reject them, with the qualification that persistence is weaker for moisture and low-level height links, and that these checks bound the rate of certain kinds of error rather than prove the links causal.

\section{Graph Properties: Sparse, Short-Lag and Regional}
\label{sec:res-properties}

Taken together, Stages A and B describe the dependence structure of BSISO-season OLR at the scale of 50 regions as follows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Sparse:} about ten links per target, of which about three come from other regions, merging into about two source regions.
  \item \textbf{Short-lag:} outside links act at one or two days; own-region memory extends to about a week.
  \item \textbf{Regional:} most outside drivers are neighbouring regions; the few remote links are carried by dynamical fields at wave speeds.
  \item \textbf{Convectively coherent:} OLR-to-OLR links dominate and are the most persistent.
\end{itemize}
This structure has a direct implication for forecasting. By the availability condition of Eq.~\eqref{eq:availability}, almost none of the outside links can be exploited directly by a seven-day forecast, whereas nearly all of them can be exploited by a one-day forecast. The graph identifies \emph{which} regions are coupled to a target; the coupling acts on time scales of a day or two.

The pattern is the same in both settings (Figure~\ref{fig:pool}). Forecast skill relative to persistence is essentially independent of the pool, at about $+0.40$, and every region beats persistence. The splitter's ability to discriminate between candidates, however, collapses as the pool grows: from 27--30 informative regions in the direct pool to 8--9 in the 2hop pool and none in the full pool. The splitter must score every candidate in one pass with a shared network, and with 50 similar-looking candidates its scores converge to a common value. Accuracy is maintained because the expert can still extract information from whichever sources are passed to it, but the selection is no longer meaningful.

This result defines the problem that the causal prior is meant to address: in a large pool, where remote drivers can be considered, the splitter alone cannot tell which candidates matter. In the project's earlier experiments on region 22, shrinking the full pool to its eight most correlated candidates did not restore discrimination, which already suggested that \emph{how} the pool is reduced matters.

The table also shows that the model loses to climatology on average at seven days ($R^2_{\mathrm{clim}}\approx-0.015$ to $-0.023$), although 14--18 of 50 regions beat it on season days. Beating persistence is a low bar at seven days, because OLR keeps almost no memory over a week; climatology is the demanding reference at this lead.

At seven days the prior has \textbf{no measurable effect on skill}. Per region, 23 improve and 27 worsen in $R^2_{\mathrm{clim}}$; the median change is $-0.010$, the mean change $-0.0002$ in $R^2_{\mathrm{clim}}$ and $+0.001$ in $S_{\mathrm{pers}}$, and the Wilcoxon signed-rank test gives $p=0.56$. The number of regions that beat climatology rises from 14 to 19, but with mean $R^2_{\mathrm{clim}}$ unchanged this reflects small movements of regions near zero rather than a systematic gain. Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:h3} is not supported.

The prior does have a large effect on \emph{source selection}: the number of informative splitters rises from 0 to 24 of 50. With the prior, the restricted pool contains the target and its prior regions, about three candidates instead of fifty, which is the regime in which the splitter can discriminate (Section~\ref{sec:res-pool}). This shows that the graph turns an uninformative full-pool splitter into one that makes distinct choices, without loss of accuracy. It is, however, expected from the pool size alone, and it does not by itself show that the graph's choice of regions is better than another choice of the same size.

\subsection{One-day lead}
At one day, the picture changes (Table~\ref{tab:stagec}, Figure~\ref{fig:stagec1}). The prior improves skill relative to persistence in \textbf{32 of 50 regions}, with a median improvement of $+0.40$ in $S_{\mathrm{pers}}$ and $+0.048$ in $R^2_{\mathrm{clim}}$; the mean $S_{\mathrm{pers}}$ improves from $-1.527$ to $-1.327$ and the mean $R^2_{\mathrm{clim}}$ from $0.720$ to $0.745$. Across regions, the Wilcoxon signed-rank test gives $p=0.049$ for $S_{\mathrm{pers}}$ and the sign test $p=0.065$. The median ratio of the expert's MSE with and without the prior is $0.83$. Region 22, the worked example, improves from $S_{\mathrm{pers}}=-0.60$ to $-0.05$. The number of informative splitters rises from 0 to 36.

Negative skill against persistence at one day is expected: OLR tomorrow is very close to OLR today, and neither model fully exploits this because the target's own history reaches the expert only when the splitter selects it. Both models are nonetheless clearly skilful relative to climatology ($R^2_{\mathrm{clim}}>0.7$ on average, 50 of 50 regions).

\subsection{Is the one-day gain due to the graph or to the smaller pool?}
Table~\ref{tab:prior-size} groups the regions by the number of outside regions in their prior. The two targets with an empty prior, which are restricted to their own region only, improve most: their mean $S_{\mathrm{pers}}$ changes from $-0.857$ to $+0.519$, and they account for two of the three regions that beat persistence with the prior. Targets with a single outside driver become worse on average, from $-1.628$ to $-2.477$, whereas targets with two or more improve.

This pattern shows that the size of the restricted pool, and in particular whether the target's own region is effectively forced into the forecast, strongly influences the outcome. A target restricted to itself is a near-persistence model, which is very hard to beat at one day. A target restricted to itself and one other region has a selected fraction of $\rho_{\mathrm{top}}=0.5$, so the splitter selects exactly one of the two, and when it selects the outside region the target's own strong persistence is lost. These effects are properties of the restricted pool, not of which regions the graph chose. Consequently, the one-day improvement over the no-prior model is established, but its attribution to the causal content of the graph requires the matched random-prior control, in which each target receives the same number of randomly chosen regions in the same mode and pool. That comparison answers RQ4 and is the first item of the continuing programme (Section~\ref{sec:conc-future}).

\subsection{Why the graph helps at one day and not at seven}
The contrast between leads follows from the graph's structure. Its outside links act at one or two days. At a one-day lead, a source region's state at the issue time is exactly the information that the link describes, so restricting the forecaster to the graph's sources concentrates it on relevant, usable information. At a seven-day lead, the same sources are coupled to the target on a time scale much shorter than the lead, and their state at the issue time says little more about the target a week later than the other candidates do. The graph identifies the right \emph{neighbours} but not the right \emph{precursors} for a week ahead. A graph built with $\taumin=7$, which tests only links usable at that lead, is the direct test of whether week-ahead precursors exist in this representation.

\subsection{Answers to RQ3 and RQ4}
For RQ3, at the seven-day lead for which the graph was intended, the validated graph does not change forecast skill, while it does make the splitter's source selection discriminative. At one day, it improves skill over the no-prior model in a majority of regions. For RQ4, the comparison with the no-prior model is established; the comparisons with random and correlation priors of equal size, which determine whether the causal content of the graph is responsible, remain to be measured. The core Phase~1 hypothesis, that a validated causal graph improves source selection \emph{and} forecast skill on unseen years, is therefore not yet confirmed: the graph is reliable, and its forecasting benefit is so far demonstrated only against the no-prior model and only at short lead.

\section{Regional and Temporal Breakdown}
\label{sec:res-breakdown}

At seven days the per-region changes are small and symmetric around zero, with no consistent geographic pattern. At one day, improvements are spread across the domain and concentrate among targets with two or more prior regions; deteriorations concentrate among targets with a single prior region. Region-by-region differences come from a single training seed and include seed-to-seed variability that has not yet been quantified. A breakdown by test year and by BSISO phase would require enough scored days per category to give stable estimates; with 1{,}989 test days over ten seasons, a year-by-year breakdown is possible but noisy, and it is included in the protocol for the multi-seed runs.

\subsection{Training budget}
With the direct pool at seven days, training for 2 epochs instead of 5 reduces the number of informative splitters from 27 to 5 of 50, while forecast skill barely changes. Discrimination therefore needs a sufficient training budget, and evaluating a selection mechanism after too little training can make it appear to fail. All Phase~1 comparisons use the same budget of 5 epochs.

\subsection{Season versus year-round training}
Restricting target days to the BSISO season changes the no-prior results only slightly (Table~\ref{tab:pool}): skill relative to persistence rises from about $+0.40$ to $+0.41$, and the number of informative splitters in the direct pool from 27 to 30. The seasonal restriction is therefore not the source of any large change in the forecasting results; its main role is to make the graph specific to the BSISO regime.

The validation pipeline itself provides ablations: removing the decoys changes the graph by 19 links (536 vs 517); removing B2 would add 3 links to the prior; B3 does not remove links at $k^\ast=10$; and B4 identifies 60 of 157 outside links as resolution-sensitive. \section{Computational Cost and Scalability}
\label{sec:res-cost}

The vectorised PCMCI makes the full-pool analysis practical: 900{,}000 candidates for 50 targets in about 23 minutes, and the complete graph pipeline, including a 550-node decoy pass and a 596-node second resolution, in about 2.5 hours on one workstation. The cost grows roughly linearly with the number of candidates per target and with the number of targets, which makes a 100-region or multi-dataset analysis feasible on the same hardware. The forecasting experiments cost about 20 minutes per 50-region configuration with 12 workers; the full 27-configuration grid is a few hours of computation, and multi-seed repetition multiplies this by the number of seeds.

\section{Error Analysis and Failure Cases}
\label{sec:res-errors}

\paragraph{Seven-day forecasts and climatology.} At seven days, every model loses to climatology on average by about 0.015--0.02 in $R^2$. A seven-day OLR anomaly in a single region of about 275\,km scale is difficult to predict, and a small model with a 4{,}000-sample training budget cannot exploit the slow, weak signals that remain. The relevant comparison in this thesis is between source-selection strategies under identical conditions, but the absolute level indicates the room that a better forecaster or a lead-matched graph has to work with.

\paragraph{Targets without outside drivers.} Regions 13 and 42 have no validated outside driver. Their restricted forecasters see only their own region, which at one day is the strongest information available and at seven days is little better than any other.

\paragraph{Targets with a single driver.} At one day, the 13 targets with one outside driver deteriorate under the restrict prior. With two candidates and $\rho_{\mathrm{top}}=0.5$, the splitter must choose between the target's own history and its single driver, and choosing the driver discards the strong persistence signal. This is a property of the interaction between the selection rule and a very small pool, and it suggests that the initialise and regularise modes, or a rule that always allows the target's own history, may be better suited to very small priors.

\paragraph{Resolution-sensitive links.} Fifteen targets fall below the persistence threshold, and PW and h850 links persist least. These links are kept in the prior with a flag. A stricter prior that excludes B4-flagged links is a further variant that can be tested with the same infrastructure.

\section{Threats to Validity and Limitations}
\label{sec:res-validity}

\paragraph{No ground truth.} The validation checks can reject a bad graph but cannot prove a graph correct. Every causal statement in this thesis is a statement about conditional dependence under the stated assumptions.

\paragraph{Statistical power.} With 18{,}000 candidates per target on strongly autocorrelated daily data, some real links will be missed and some false links retained, even with FDR control.

\paragraph{Hidden common drivers.} PCMCI assumes that all common drivers are among the nodes. BSISO phase, ENSO, extratropical influences and sub-regional moisture processes are only partly captured by 300 regional means.

\paragraph{Field choice within a region.} The fields of a region move together with the same wave, so which field PCMCI selects can change with small changes in the data. Claims are therefore made at the level of source regions.

\paragraph{Partly circular regions.} Regions were clustered on OLR co-variability and then used to find OLR links. The effect is to make between-region OLR links harder to find, but the partition is not independent of the target.

\paragraph{Climatology overlap.} The upstream anomaly climatology was computed over 1979--2014, which overlaps the test period by two years. All compared models share it, so the comparisons between them are unaffected, but absolute skill values may be very slightly optimistic.

\paragraph{Single seed.} All forecasting comparisons use one training seed. Per-region differences of the size seen at seven days are within the range that seed variability can produce, and the one-day differences, although consistent across most regions, should be confirmed with several seeds.

\paragraph{Weak seven-day skill.} At seven days the models lose to climatology on average, so the comparison between priors takes place in a regime of low absolute skill.

\paragraph{Region-scale statistical claims.} A statement such as "h850 in region 27 drives OLR in region 22 at one to three days" is a statistical statement about region means, not a description of a physical mechanism.

\paragraph{External validity.} The graph is estimated from one data archive, one partition and one season definition. Its stability across data sources, time periods and the full year is part of the continuing programme.

\section{Implications for Phase 2}
\label{sec:res-implications}

The Phase~1 results lead to three groups of conclusions for the India-focused extension:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Keep:} the validation pipeline (decoys, physical lag mask and resolution persistence), which runs at scale and gives interpretable diagnostics; the activity-based season restriction for target days; and a region-level prior, which matches the granularity at which source claims are reliable.
  \item \textbf{Change:} build the graph with lags matched to the forecast horizon ($\taumin\ge L$); select the prior size by forecast-horizon skill or by comparison with random sources; and keep the target's own history available when the prior is very small.
  \item \textbf{Do not yet rely on:} set-based causal-signature distance from short windows as a trigger for expert adaptation; error-based drift signals, or strength-based structural distances over longer windows, are better candidates.
\end{itemize}

\section{Chapter Summary}
\label{sec:res-summary}

The Phase~1 graph is sparse, short-lag and largely regional, and it passes every validation check that can reject it: no decoy is selected, 3 of 536 links violate physical speed limits, and 62\% of outside links persist at doubled resolution. Used as a restrict prior in the full pool, it makes the splitter's source scores discriminative at both leads. It leaves seven-day skill unchanged and improves one-day skill over the no-prior model in 32 of 50 regions. Whether the one-day gain reflects the graph's choice of regions, rather than the reduced pool, is determined by the matched random-prior control. The causal structure estimated from short windows does not track ENSO. These results answer RQ1 and RQ2 positively, answer RQ3 with a lead-dependent result, leave RQ4 open, and inform RQ5, which the next chapter develops.
